Coverage in Writing

Warranty

Every installation we complete carries two layers of coverage: the manufacturer's warranty on the window or door itself, and our own written workmanship warranty on the installation. Both are documented, both are honored by our own office, and both are explained to you before you sign.

Two Layers of Coverage

A window or door problem is not always a product problem — sometimes it is an installation problem. Knowing which warranty applies is what gets the issue resolved quickly and at no cost to you.

Manufacturer Product Warranty

Covers
The window or door unit itself — frame, sash, hardware, and insulated glass
Typical defects
Failed insulated glass seal, defective hardware, frame or sash defects, finish defects
Term
Set by the manufacturer and stated on the printed warranty issued with your order
Who honors it
The manufacturer — and we file and manage the claim on your behalf

Our Workmanship Warranty

Covers
The installation itself — flashing, sealing, fastening, insulation, and finish
Typical defects
Leaks at the rough opening, air infiltration at the perimeter, loose or incorrect fastening, finish damage from our crews
Term
Stated in writing in your contract at the time of sale
Who honors it
Mid-Florida Windows and Doors, LLC — our own crews return to correct it

Request the manufacturer's printed warranty and our written workmanship terms for your specific product line before you sign anything.

What Is Covered

The workmanship warranty is the part we control directly, so it is worth being specific about what falls inside it.

  • Water intrusion at the opening. Leaks caused by improper flashing, panning, or sealant application around the rough opening are corrected at no charge.
  • Air infiltration at the perimeter. Drafts between the unit and the wall that result from incomplete insulation or sealing.
  • Fastening and anchoring. Loose units, incorrect fastener type or spacing, and failures of the attachment to the structure.
  • Operation and adjustment. Sashes, rollers, and lock hardware that do not operate smoothly because of installation or that need adjustment after settling.
  • Level, plumb, and square. Units installed out of square, causing binding or visible gaps, are corrected.
  • Interior and exterior finish. Drywall, stucco edge, trim, and paint damage caused by our installation crews.
  • Insulated glass seal failure. Fogging or condensation between panes — a product defect we file with the manufacturer for you.
  • Hardware defects. Failed locks, hinges, handles, and cranks handled under the manufacturer warranty with our labor included.

One call is all it takes. If you are not sure whether your issue is a product defect or a workmanship issue, call our office and describe it. We inspect, we determine which warranty applies, and we handle it from there — you do not have to chase the manufacturer yourself.

What Is Not Covered

Being clear about exclusions up front is part of an honest warranty. These are the items outside warranty coverage.

  • Accidental glass breakage. A cracked or shattered pane from impact, a projectile, or a storm is not a defect. This may be covered by your homeowners insurance instead.
  • Storm and wind-borne damage. Damage from hurricane, tornado, or flying debris is an insurance matter, not a warranty claim.
  • Unauthorized modification. Grilles, films, tinting, alarms, or hardware installed by a third party can affect coverage.
  • Work by others. Repairs or adjustments performed by someone other than us on our installation.
  • Maintenance neglect. Failed sealant that was never renewed, or weep holes left clogged, as sealant and drainage are wear items.
  • Normal wear and appearance. Fading of exterior finishes, weathering of caulk lines, and screen fabric wear over time.

Coverage questions are answered in writing before you sign. If a term is unclear, ask us and we will put the answer in your contract.

Keeping Your Warranty in Good Standing

Warranties on windows and doors are easy to maintain. A few minutes twice a year protects both the product and your coverage.

Keep weep holes clear

Every window and door frame has small drainage openings at the bottom. Leaves, dirt, and paint block them and trap water. Check them every spring and fall and clear them with a soft brush or a piece of wire.

Renew exterior sealant as it ages

Caulk lines around the perimeter are a wear item in Florida's sun. Inspect them annually and have them renewed when they crack or pull away. This is the single most common cause of leaks we see on otherwise sound installations.

Keep the documentation

Store the manufacturer's printed warranty and your contract in your home records. If you sell the property, pass them along. If you lose them, call us — we keep records tied to your installation address and can help you recover them.

Warranty FAQ

Does my new window or door come with a warranty?

Yes, and there are two separate layers. The manufacturer warrants the window or door product itself, covering defects in materials and workmanship in the unit. Separately, Mid-Florida Windows and Doors provides a written workmanship warranty covering the installation itself: flashing, sealing, fastening, insulation, and finish work. You receive the manufacturer's printed warranty documents along with our written workmanship terms at the time of installation.

What does the workmanship warranty cover?

The workmanship warranty covers defects in how the products were installed rather than defects in the products themselves. That includes water intrusion at the rough opening caused by improper flashing or sealing, air infiltration at the perimeter, insecure or incorrect fastening, improper shimming or leveling, damage to the interior or exterior finish caused by our crews, and hardware adjustments needed after installation.

How long is the warranty and does it transfer if I sell my home?

Warranty length differs by product line and is stated on the manufacturer documents issued for your specific order. Many of the impact and vinyl product lines we install carry limited lifetime coverage on the frame and sash, with defined terms for hardware, glass, and seal failure. Workmanship coverage terms are stated in writing in your contract. Ask us for the exact term for the product line you are considering and we will give it to you before you sign.

Does a broken window or a leaking door void the warranty?

Accidental breakage such as a cracked pane from an impact is not a defect and is generally not covered. Likewise, warranty coverage can be affected by unauthorized modifications, by work performed by a third party on the unit, or by failure to perform reasonable maintenance such as keeping weep holes clear and re-caulking as sealant ages. Ordinary wear items, glass breakage from storms or projectiles, and damage from neglect are typically excluded.

How do I file a warranty claim?

Call our office at (888) 230-0243 or (727) 432-1899 and describe the problem. Have your installation date and, if you have it, your contract or invoice number available. We will schedule an inspection. If the issue is a workmanship matter we correct it as a warranty service call. If it is a product defect we handle the manufacturer claim process on your behalf, including ordering replacement parts or units and performing the labor.

Is my warranty still valid if I sell the house?

Most of the manufacturer warranties on the product lines we install are transferable to a subsequent homeowner, and transfer terms are set out in the manufacturer's printed warranty for your product. Workmanship coverage terms are stated in your contract. When you sell, pass along the warranty documentation we provided at installation. If you have misplaced it, contact us and we will help you recover the record for your address.
