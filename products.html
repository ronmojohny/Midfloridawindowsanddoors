Impact vs. Non-Impact: Which Is Right for You?

This is the decision customers ask about most. It is not always all-or-nothing — many Central Florida homes use a mix of both.

Impact Windows & Doors

  • Storm protection. Laminated glass and reinforced frames resist wind-borne debris.
  • Possible insurance credits. Many Florida carriers offer wind mitigation discounts for impact-rated units.
  • No shutters to deploy. Protection is built in, so you are ready when a storm turns.
  • Noise reduction. The laminated interlayer noticeably softens outside sound.
  • Higher upfront cost. Impact assemblies cost more per opening than non-impact.

Non-Impact Windows & Doors

  • Lower cost per opening. More of the home can be upgraded within the same budget.
  • Strong energy performance. Insulated low-E glass reduces solar heat gain.
  • Clean sightlines. Lighter sash profiles maximize the view.
  • Requires storm shutters. You still need panels or shutters for full protection.
  • No wind mitigation credit. Generally does not qualify for impact-based insurance discounts.

There is no single right answer. On exposed elevations facing open water or a golf course, impact pays off. On protected interior-facing openings, non-impact often delivers the best value. We will walk your home, look at exposures, and give you an honest recommendation — not a one-size-fits-all pitch.

Product FAQ

What should I choose in Florida, impact or non-impact windows?

Impact windows use laminated glass and reinforced frames to resist wind-borne debris, and are often the right choice in coastal and High Velocity Hurricane Zone areas, where they may also reduce homeowners insurance premiums. Non-impact windows use standard insulated glass, cost less per opening, and deliver strong energy performance. Many Central Florida homes are a mix: impact on exposed elevations and non-impact where protection is less critical. We will walk your home and tell you honestly which openings benefit most.

Do new windows and doors really lower energy bills?

Windows and doors are among the largest sources of heat gain in a Florida home. Modern units with insulated low-emissivity glass, warm-edge spacers, and properly sealed frames reduce solar heat gain and air infiltration, which takes pressure off your air conditioning. Actual savings depend on the age and condition of your current units, your sun exposure, and how well your home is insulated.

Can you match the look of my existing windows and doors?

Yes. We offer a range of frame colors and finishes, grid patterns, glass tints, and door styles. During your free in-home consultation we compare your existing units and help you select a profile, grid, and finish that fits the architecture of your home and your neighborhood.

Will new windows and doors reduce my homeowners insurance?

Impact-rated windows and doors commonly qualify for wind mitigation credits with Florida insurers because they reduce the risk of breach during a storm. Credits vary by carrier and by the other features of your home. Ask your agent what documentation they need, and we will provide the product approval numbers and installation details for your units.

How long does a window and door project take?

Most residential replacement projects are measured in days, not weeks, once materials arrive. Product lead times vary by manufacturer and by how custom your order is. During your consultation we give you a realistic schedule for your specific product lines and a clear installation window.

Yes. A product specialist brings samples and literature to your home so you can see and handle frame profiles, glass samples, and door styles in your own lighting. Call (888) 230-0243 to schedule a free in-home consultation.
